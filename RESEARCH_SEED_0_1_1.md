# Research Seed 0.1.1

**Repository:** Invariant Structure Research  
**Status:** exploratory / preservation-first  
**Epistemic posture:** hypothesis-generating, comparison-driven, non-unification-presuming

## 1. Origin

This research space arose from a portfolio-level reflection on several long-running projects that initially appeared to belong to very different domains.

A first explanation for their relationship was genealogical: one project led historically to another.

A deeper observation then emerged:

> All of these projects exist in one and the same global universe.

They therefore do not inhabit independent realities merely because they are separated by academic discipline, software repository, vocabulary, or scale.

This does **not** establish that they share one theory or one mechanism.

It does motivate asking whether some apparently domain-specific structures are actually recurring mathematical or relational forms.

## 2. The motivating distinction

There are at least three different kinds of inter-project relationship:

### Genealogical

One project historically led to another.

### Operational

One project exists partly to support, test, observe, or preserve another.

### Ontological

All projects, investigators, machines, mathematical models, physical systems, institutions, markets, and artifacts exist within one reality and therefore cannot ultimately be exempt from the same global consistency requirement.

The third relationship is the principal motivation for this repository.

## 3. Central caution

Ontological unity does **not** imply evidential interchangeability.

For example:

- successful software architecture does not validate a cosmological theory;
- a cryptographic construction does not establish a physical mechanism;
- a profitable or robust market effect does not establish a cosmological claim;
- a theological argument does not become an experimental physics result;
- a shared graph, lattice, symmetry, grammar, state machine, or robustness pattern does not establish common causation.

Cross-domain reasoning must therefore distinguish shared structure from shared mechanism.

## 4. Governing research question

> Which mathematical or structural invariants recur across apparently distinct domains, and which apparent correspondences survive rigorous attempts to distinguish genuine shared structure from analogy or coincidence?

## 5. Candidate recurring structures

The initial conversation identified the following candidate recurring structures:

- symmetry;
- invariance;
- lattices;
- graphs;
- adjacency;
- networks;
- local/global relationships;
- hierarchy;
- nesting;
- state transitions;
- admissibility;
- constraints;
- canonical representation;

- equivalence versus identity;
- boundary conditions;
- information preservation;

- reversibility / irreversibility;

With the inclusion of Evidence-Based Market Methods, additional attention is warranted for:

- resource constraints and decision relevance;

This list is a search space, not a result.

## 6. Candidate higher-order pattern

A possible higher-order recurring form is:

> **structured multiplicity constrained by invariant relations**

A related candidate pattern is:

> **local representation → transformation → invariant / global state**

Examples may exist in crystallography, physics, scientific comparison, project continuity, software state, distributed authority, and empirical market research.

At seed stage, these are organizing hypotheses only.

## 7. Representation versus invariant reality

Another recurring theme is the distinction between a representation and what it represents.

Different agents, coordinate systems, grammars, software views, scientific frameworks, historical summaries, empirical signals, or market classifications may describe the same underlying state differently.

The research question is therefore not merely whether two projects use similar words.

It is whether there exists a defensible relationship among:

- represented objects;
- transformations among representations;
- invariant quantities or relations;
- and the reality claims those representations are allowed to make.

## 8. Initial project set

### Nested Fibrational Cosmology (NFC)

Potential structures of interest include nesting, fibers/spines, triality, adjacency, regime structure, persistence, local/global behavior, and crystallographic interpretations.

No NFC claim is adopted here merely because it supplies suggestive structure.

### Physical Grammar Hypothesis (PGH)

Potential structures include grammar, admissible composition, rule systems, state generation, and alternate descriptions of physical organization.

Its relationship to NFC is not assumed.

### Foundational Convergence Program (FCP)

Potential structures include framework normalization, equal-standard comparison, invariants across descriptions, admissibility gates, evidential boundaries, and surviving claims.

FCP may also provide methodological discipline for testing cross-domain bridges.

### Project Observatory

Potential structures include state reconstruction, dependency relations, normalization, stale/current distinctions, graph-like project organization, and observer-versus-state problems.

### Project Continuity Research (PCR)

Potential structures include canonical identity, authority, state transition, succession, evidence persistence, local/global orientation, representation drift, and recoverability under changing agents.

### HiVenues

Potential structures include canonical host state, multiple faithful renderings, draft/live transitions, authorization, identity, graph relationships, cryptographic infrastructure, and local host-specific presentation over wider network state.

### Evidence-Based Market Methods (EBMM)

The project is a continuity-first empirical market-research repository whose current mutable state is canonically represented in `STATE.yaml`, with stable governance separated from current conclusions and Git history preserving prior states.

Potential structures of interest include:

- canonical mutable state;
- evidence / synthesis / decision separation;
- screening and admissibility rules;
- family/subfamily hierarchy;
- robustness across instruments, periods, regimes, and implementation conditions;
- benchmark-relative evaluation;
- holdout and forward-test boundaries;
- multiple-testing and data-snooping controls;
- cost, slippage, financing, and implementation constraints;
- negative-result preservation;
- a strict research-to-capital authorization boundary.

EBMM is especially useful as a comparison domain because its invariants, where they exist, are empirical and decision-relevant rather than primarily geometric or software-architectural.

No market result is treated here as evidence for any unrelated physical or software claim.

## 9. Interdisciplinary precedent

The motivating discussion used crystallography and modern cryptography as an example of why disciplinary labels can obscure shared mathematics.

The point is not that crystallography historically created cryptography.

The point is that structures such as lattices, symmetry, discrete transformations, and geometry can become central in multiple fields for different reasons.

EBMM broadens this motivation: recurring structure need not always be geometric. It may instead appear as robustness, canonical state, controlled transformation, admissibility, invariance under perturbation, or preservation of decision-relevant information.

This motivates searching below the domain-label level.

## 10. Bridge classification

Every proposed relationship should receive one provisional classification:

### `EXACT`

The same formally defined structure or relationship is present under an explicit mapping strong enough to justify exact correspondence within the stated scope.

### `FORMAL_ANALOGUE`

The systems share a mathematically or structurally meaningful form, but identity or mechanism is not established.

### `POSSIBLE_BRIDGE`

There is enough structure to justify a targeted investigation of a stronger relationship, but the bridge has not been established.

### `SUPERFICIAL_SIMILARITY`

The resemblance is primarily linguistic, visual, thematic, or low-dimensional and does not survive structural analysis.

### `NOT_ESTABLISHED`

Available evidence is insufficient to classify the proposed relationship more strongly.

Classification is local to the specific claim. A pair of projects may have one exact correspondence and many unrelated features.

## 11. Required bridge test

A proposed bridge must answer:

### A. Objects

What exact entities, states, relations, or spaces correspond?

### B. Mapping

What function, transformation, functor, encoding, reduction, correspondence, or other explicit relation connects them?

### C. Invariants

What remains unchanged or predictably transformed?

### D. Falsification

What result would distinguish a substantive correspondence from analogy, coincidence, or overfitting?

A proposed bridge that cannot yet answer these questions should remain `NOT_ESTABLISHED` or `POSSIBLE_BRIDGE`.

## 12. Prohibited shortcuts

Do not infer:

- common mechanism from common terminology;
- physical truth from mathematical elegance;
- causal relation from isomorphism;
- universality from one regime;
- scientific support from software success;
- scientific support from market profitability;
- cross-project validation from historical genealogy;
- truth from recurrence alone.

Do not retrofit every project into a preferred framework.

## 13. Preservation-first scope

At version 0.1.1 this repository is intentionally lightweight.

It exists to preserve and examine the emerging hypothesis before conversation context is lost.

It is **not yet authorized by its own existence** to become a large autonomous program with elaborate phases, governance, infrastructure, or implementation.

Expansion should be earned by research results.

## 14. Initial method

1. Extract the actual formal or operational structures from each project.
2. Avoid relying on project slogans or shared vocabulary.
3. Populate the Structural Crosswalk.
4. Identify candidate pairwise correspondences.
5. Apply the bridge test.
6. Attempt to falsify the strongest-looking correspondences.
7. Promote only relationships that survive.
8. Preserve negative results as first-class evidence.

## 15. Seed thesis

The seed thesis is deliberately weak:

> Apparently distinct projects may instantiate recurring mathematical or structural forms because they operate within one reality, but every claimed cross-domain relationship must be independently established rather than inferred from that shared reality.

That proposition is sufficient to begin investigation without presuming the outcome.
